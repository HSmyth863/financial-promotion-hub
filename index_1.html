Back-End Frictions Compliance

"Back-end frictions" are deliberate friction points or safeguards built into the customer journey after a user has seen a financial promotion, but before they are allowed to engage with a high-risk investment (like cryptoassets). These frictions are designed to slow down impulsive action, ensure the client is suitably informed, and that they meet the regulatory criteria for investing. This guide provides a breakdown of what that includes under FCA rules.

⬥ Pre-investment safeguards required by the FCA
⬥ Introduced under PS23/6
⬥ Codified across COBS 4.12A and COBS 10A
What These Frictions Ensure
  • › The consumer is suitable (via categorisation)
  • › The product is appropriate (via assessment)
  • › The decision is considered (via delay mechanisms)
1
Cooling-Off Period
COBS 4.12A.18R
▾

A 24-hour pause must follow successful categorisation and assessment. Users cannot trade or invest during this time. The purpose is to encourage reflection and reduce impulsive behaviour.

FCA Requirement
COBS 4.12A.18R: A firm must not communicate a direct-offer financial promotion to a retail client unless at least 24 hours have elapsed since the client first requested to receive such a promotion.
How It Works in Practice
  • The 24 hours begins from the moment a user requests to receive direct-offer financial promotions — typically when they tap 'Sign Up' or 'Get Started' on your platform.
  • During the 24-hour period, users can complete other onboarding steps (KYC, client categorisation, appropriateness assessment) but they must not be able to access any trading functionality.
  • Once the 24 hours has elapsed, the user must be presented with a clear screen offering two equally prominent options: to proceed or to leave the investment journey.
  • A longer cooling-off period is acceptable — the FCA minimum is 24 hours. However, the proceed/leave screen is still required when that period ends.
Automated Message Example
"As part of your onboarding, you are now in a mandatory 24-hour cooling-off period. This gives you time to consider whether cryptoasset trading is right for you. Access to trading will be available from [TIME] on [DATE+1], once all onboarding steps are complete."
UI Suggestion
Include a visible countdown timer or timestamp, showing the user how much time remains.

Appropriateness Assessment
COBS 4.12A.27G / COBS 10 Annex 1
▾
Key Requirements
  • Questions must cover all items listed in COBS 10 Annex 4G.
  • A minimum of 10 questions must be included per assessment.
  • Each set of questions must be unique to prevent repetition across attempts.
  • An 80% pass rate is advised.
  • Do not call this a "quiz"; label it as Appropriateness Assessment.
  • The number of attempts available to a user must equal the number of question banks created.
  • If a user fails all available attempts, they must be informed that cryptoasset investment is not suitable for them and they cannot proceed with onboarding.
  • After a second failed attempt, a user must wait 24 hours before making any further attempts.
Best Practices
  • Integrate into your onboarding UI.
  • Include educational resources for all attempts (whether pass or fail).
  • Enforce increasing cooldowns to protect assessment integrity. E.g. After a user fails twice, they must wait 24 hours before attempting again; after third, 36 hours; fourth, 48 hours.
Compliance Tip
Keep durable records of each assessment attempt.

Monitoring, Compliance and Reporting Process
Ongoing Obligation
▾

Firms must monitor client behaviour to ensure it aligns with their categorisation.

  • Example: If a user claiming to be a restricted investor engages in high-volume or risky trades, this may trigger re-categorisation protocols.
  • All back-end actions (including friction events) must be logged for compliance.
  • Continuously monitor user trading activity to ensure it aligns with investor category.
  • Maintain and review all onboarding flows and disclosures against latest FCA updates.
  • Keep a detailed compliance log of all onboarding components: categorisations, assessments, cooling-off periods, and risk warnings.
Why It Matters
These non-optional back-end frictions demonstrate that a firm is:
  • › Not onboarding recklessly
  • › Taking steps to educate clients
  • › Preventing harmful behaviour
  • › Compliant with FCA's requirements for cryptoasset promotions

Cryptoasset Promotions
General Cryptoasset Promotion Compliance

A practical reference for preparing compliant UK cryptoasset financial promotions across any medium — websites, emails, in-app screens, social posts, brochures and beyond. Covers the four cornerstones every promotion must meet before submission to Zeyro: the risk warning banner, the approval time stamp, balancing language, and the fair, clear and not misleading standard.

Applies to Every Medium
The principles in this guide apply to all UK cryptoasset financial promotions — websites, mobile apps, App Store and Play Store listings, social media posts, email campaigns, paid advertising, brochures, decks, push notifications, and any other communication that invites or induces a UK user to engage in cryptoasset activity. The medium changes the implementation; the underlying obligations do not.

Who Reviews It
Zeyro Ltd (FRN 1001386) is the s21 approver of your financial promotions. Zeyro reviews each promotion against the FCA cryptoasset financial promotion regime — principally COBS 4.12A, COBS 4.2 and COBS 4.10 — and the broader requirement that every financial promotion be fair, clear and not misleading. The FCA does not approve individual promotions; Zeyro does, and the approval time stamp on your promotion must reflect that.

00
Scope & UK Users
FSMA 2000 s.21 · COBS 4.3.1R
▾

The UK cryptoasset financial promotion regime applies to any communication that is an invitation or inducement to engage in investment activity and is capable of having effect in the UK. Before applying the four cornerstones, firms should be clear on which of their content is in scope and how UK users are identified.

Identifying UK Users

The primary method for identifying UK users should be geolocation via IP detection. Citizenship alone is less reliable — a UK citizen living abroad would typically be subject to the regulations of their country of residence. Firms must have a clear, documented mechanism for identifying and applying UK regulatory requirements to the correct user cohort, and Zeyro will typically ask how this is implemented during review.

What Counts as a Financial Promotion

A financial promotion is not limited to dedicated marketing pages or paid adverts. In a cryptoasset context, in-scope communications routinely include:

  • Web & landing pages — home pages, product pages, pricing pages, and any page describing assets, services, rates or features
  • App screens — pre-login, sign-up, dashboard, asset listings, product flows, account centres, transaction reviews
  • App Store / Play Store listings — description text and accompanying screenshots
  • Social media — organic posts, paid ads, influencer content, pinned posts, profile bios where they make benefit claims
  • Email & CRM — marketing emails, transactional emails that include feature promotions, push notifications
  • Sales collateral — brochures, decks, factsheets, partner microsites
Not a Financial Promotion
Pure brand awareness content with no product information (for example "Firm X" and logo image-only ads), and certain service-based communications that contain no benefit claims, rate quotes or product descriptions, are generally outside the regime. If in doubt, include the content in your submission and Zeyro will confirm its status.
How This Guide Is Structured

The four cornerstones that follow apply to every in-scope communication. Where the medium imposes a specific implementation challenge (for example, a fixed banner in an app, a character-limited social post, or a static App Store screenshot), refer to the medium-specific guides on the Financial Promotion Hub for the additional points that apply.

1
Risk Warning Banner
COBS 4.12A.10R · COBS 4.12A.11R · COBS 4.12A.39G
▾

Every financial promotion for a qualifying cryptoasset that is directed at UK users must contain the FCA prescribed risk warning. This is non-negotiable and is the first item Zeyro checks when reviewing a submission. The wording is prescribed verbatim and cannot be paraphrased, abbreviated or restyled.

Prescribed Wording (COBS 4.12A.11R)
"Don't invest unless you're prepared to lose all the money you invest. This is a high-risk investment and you should not expect to be protected if something goes wrong. Take 2 mins to learn more"
Placement

The banner must be placed at the top of the promotion, before any other promotional content. No other text should sit inside the banner itself — it must stand alone so that the user sees the warning before any feature claims or marketing copy. On web and in-app environments, the banner should remain visible as the user scrolls through the page or screen, not just at initial load.

The "Take 2 mins to learn more" Link

Where the medium supports hyperlinks, the call-to-action must be embedded as a clickable link to your Risk Summary page, which itself must follow the structure set out in COBS 4 Annex 1R. For static contexts (print materials, certain App Store fields, image-only visuals), a typed-out URL is acceptable — but the link text itself must remain exactly "Take 2 mins to learn more". Do not rephrase the call-to-action, and do not require the user to click through multiple intermediate links to reach the risk summary.

Prominence & Design Limits

Under COBS 4.12A.39G, the promotion must not contain any design feature that has the intent or effect of reducing the visibility or prominence of the risk warning or risk summary. The following are treated as prominence failures:

  • Using a font size that is smaller than the standard size used elsewhere in the promotion
  • Using a background colour that does not sufficiently contrast with the text, or that reduces the visibility of the text
  • Fading, greying out or otherwise de-emphasising the text of the warning or summary
  • Placing the risk warning at the bottom of the promotion, or embedding it within other standard information
  • Requiring additional links to be clicked in order for the full text of the warning or summary to be seen
  • Using a font or background in the warning or summary that matches the firm's brand colours, or that matches the colour used for other forms of disclosure
  • Making the banner dismissible with a close (×) button, or allowing it to scroll off-screen in a digital environment

Approval Time Stamp
COBS 4.10.7A
▾

Under COBS 4.10.7A, every financial promotion must display, with sufficient prominence, the name (or FRN) of the approving firm and the date on which it was approved. All UK financial promotions approved by Zeyro must include the following approval time stamp.

Prescribed Wording
"This Financial Promotion has been approved by Zeyro LTD (FRN 1001386) on [DATE]"
Placement

The approval time stamp should appear at the bottom of the promotion, clearly legible and not embedded within other text. Treat it as a standalone closing line — not a clause inside a footer block or a sentence buried in the terms & conditions. In digital environments, the stamp must appear on every page or screen that carries the risk warning banner.

Date & Wording
  • Replace [DATE] with the actual date Zeyro provided approval — never leave a placeholder, generic year, or "TBC"
  • The wording must refer specifically to a "financial promotion" — not an "offer", "advert", "listing", "content" or any other term
  • If your firm was previously approved under a different approver name (for example, Englebert Ltd), update the stamp to Zeyro Ltd (FRN 1001386) going forward
  • Do not abbreviate the FRN, and do not add or remove the "LTD" suffix — the wording is prescribed
Updates & Resubmissions
If you make material changes to a promotion after approval, the updated version will need to be resubmitted to Zeyro for review prior to being applied. Where the change is minor and remains within the scope of the originally approved content, the original approval date can typically be retained — Zeyro will confirm this on a case-by-case basis. Do not update the live time stamp date without written confirmation.
"FCA-Approved" — Avoid Entirely

Do not describe a promotion — or your firm more broadly — as "FCA-approved", "FCA-endorsed" or "FCA-compliant". The FCA does not approve financial promotions; Zeyro Ltd, as your s21 approver, approves them. Any phrasing implying FCA endorsement is misleading under COBS 4.2.1R and GEN 4.5.3, and will be flagged for removal at review.

Balancing Language
COBS 4.2.1R — Fair, Clear & Not Misleading
▾

While the prescribed Risk Warning Banner addresses the general risks of qualifying cryptoassets, additional risk language must be incorporated within the body of the promotion to appropriately balance the benefits highlighted. The general banner is not a substitute for contextualised risk disclosure alongside specific claims. The key question to ask is: for every benefit described here, is there a corresponding risk the user needs to understand? Where the answer is no, balancing language must be added in proximity to the relevant claim — not only in the footer, the risk summary, or a separate page.

Core Principles of Equal Prominence
  • Risk disclosures must be presented with equal prominence to the benefits they accompany — matching font size, weight, contrast and placement
  • Risk information must not be hidden, de-emphasised, or grouped at the bottom of a page or screen where a user may never see it
  • Risk language must be specific to the feature being described — generic disclaimers are not sufficient where specific assets or services are promoted
  • Risk language placed in a hover tooltip, behind a "learn more" link, or only in the T&Cs is not acceptable as the primary disclosure
Asset-Specific Risks

Where benefits or features of specific cryptoasset types are highlighted, they must be balanced with clear explanations of the associated risks. A general risk warning is not sufficient where specific asset categories — DeFi tokens, memecoins, stablecoins, wrapped tokens, governance tokens, security tokens — are being promoted. Each carries its own risk profile that must be surfaced where that asset type is featured.

Service-Specific Risks

Specific services carry distinct risks that must be disclosed alongside the benefit claim. Each of the following requires tailored risk language wherever the service is mentioned in the promotion:

APY & Yield Figures

Headline rates are one of the most frequently flagged items in Zeyro reviews. Where a rate, APY or yield figure appears anywhere in the promotion, the following must be presented immediately adjacent to it — not behind a "learn more" link, an asterisk that resolves only in the T&Cs, or in a footer:

  • Variability — that rates can change, with a clear explanation of what "up to" means in practice and an indication of typical or current rates
  • Calculation basis — how the APY or yield is calculated and what assumptions it is built on
  • Effective date & source — the date the rate was current as at, and the in-app source or data provider
  • Promotional vs standard — where the rate is promotional or introductory, the duration and end-conditions
  • Annualisation — where staking or yield periods are shorter than a year, make clear the figure is an annualised representation, not what the user will necessarily receive
Comparative & Superlative Claims
Phrases like "industry-leading reward rates", "the best rates on the market", or "lower fees than X" are particularly scrutinised and require substantiation — clear methodology, comparison cohort, data source and a recent effective date — visible adjacent to the claim. Where the claim cannot be substantiated, it must be removed or reframed.
4
Fair, Clear & Not Misleading
COBS 4.2.1R · COBS 4.2.5G · GEN 4.5.3
▾

The overarching obligation for every UK financial promotion is that it must be fair, clear and not misleading. This sits above the specific requirements covered in cornerstones 1–3 and is the lens through which Zeyro reviews every submission. Three sub-principles drive most of the feedback firms receive on this point.

Fair

A promotion is fair where it provides a balanced representation of both risks and benefits, and does not omit or obscure material information that could influence a consumer's understanding or decision-making. In practice that means:

  • All relevant risks associated with the product are clearly disclosed, including prominent risk warnings
  • Exaggerated or unsubstantiated claims ("guaranteed returns", "low risk") are removed
  • Comparisons or references to other products are objective and substantiated, with a clear methodology and data source
  • Fees, charges and the basis of any advertised rate are disclosed prominently — not buried in T&Cs
Clear

Clarity ensures that the information presented is easy to understand for the target audience. In a cryptoasset context this typically means:

  • Plain, straightforward language — jargon and technical terms are explained where used
  • Content organised so that key information (especially risk warnings) is not buried, hidden or split across multiple screens
  • Risk disclosures immediately visible without scrolling on digital formats; on the same screen as the related claim
  • Visual prominence — risk language shares font size, weight and contrast with the benefit language it balances
Not Misleading

Promotions must not mislead by action or omission. The most common misleading patterns Zeyro flags are:

  • Selective presentation of information that highlights positive aspects while downplaying associated risks
  • Headlines like "Earn up to 50%" without an equally prominent qualifier on variability and capital risk
  • Implying immediate or instant access to trading where mandatory back-end frictions (personalised risk warning, categorisation, appropriateness assessment, 24-hour cooling-off) actually apply
  • Customer-journey diagrams or "4 simple steps" that omit the frictions flow
  • References to overseas regulatory status without a UK-protection clarification of equal prominence
  • Claims implying FCA endorsement, FCA approval, or that crypto products are covered by FSCS or FOS

Sensitive Words & Framing
COBS 4.2.5G
▾

Under COBS 4.2.5G, the terms "guaranteed", "secure" and "protected" are treated as sensitive words by the FCA. Where these terms (or related ones such as "safe", "risk-free", "trusted") diminish the complexities and risks of cryptoassets, imply certainty and safety of capital, or imply regulatory or compensation protection, they should be removed. Where they are being used to describe a specific technical process or safeguard, the FCA expects clarity, qualification and substantiation in each instance.

The Risk Warning Banner
COBS 4.12A.10R / COBS 4.12A.11R / COBS 4.12A.38G
▾
1.1 Why the Risk Warning Banner Matters

Under COBS 4.12A.10R and COBS 4.12A.11R, every page of your website that constitutes a financial promotion must display the FCA-prescribed risk warning. This is non-negotiable and is the first thing Zeyro checks when reviewing a submission.

1.2 The Prescribed Wording

You must use the following exact wording. It cannot be paraphrased, shortened (unless character limits genuinely prevent it), or have other text inserted within the banner:

Prescribed Wording
"Don't invest unless you're prepared to lose all the money you invest. This is a high-risk investment and you should not expect to be protected if something goes wrong. Take 2 mins to learn more"

Source: COBS 4.12A.11R. This wording must appear verbatim on every page that is a financial promotion.

1.3 The "Take 2 Mins to Learn More" Link

Immediately following the prescribed risk warning, you must include a clickable link with the text "Take 2 mins to learn more" (COBS 4.12A.11R(3)). This link must open your risk summary page — either as a pop-up, a dedicated page on your domain, or an equivalent.

  • The link text must be exactly "Take 2 mins to learn more" — do not rephrase it.
  • The risk summary content must follow the structure set out in COBS 4 Annex 1R.
  • The link must be part of the risk warning banner — not elsewhere on the page.
1.4 Placement — Where the Banner Must Appear

The risk warning banner must be placed at the top of every page below any other fixed elements (such as a navigation bar). It must remain fixed and visible at all times — it must not scroll off screen as the user moves down the page.

  • Apply the banner to every page that a UK user can access, including log-in and registration pages.
  • The banner must stay fixed at the top of the screen when users scroll — this is a common issue we flag.
  • Do not place the risk warning only at the bottom of the page.
1.5 Design and Prominence Requirements

The FCA is very clear that the risk warning must not be diminished by design choices. Under COBS 4.12A.38G and COBS 4.12A.40R, the following design failures will result in rejection:

Design Failures That Will Be Rejected
  • ✗ Using a font size smaller than the standard text size used elsewhere on the page
  • ✗ Using a background colour that does not sufficiently contrast with the text
  • ✗ Making the risk warning dismissible — do not include an 'X' or close button
  • ✗ Fading the text of the risk warning
  • ✗ Placing the risk warning behind an additional click (e.g. requiring users to expand it)
  • ✗ Using a font or background colour that blends with the surrounding content
  • ✗ Allowing the warning to scroll off the screen when the user scrolls down
Real Example From Our Review
One client used black text against a dark grey background for the risk warning. The FCA flagged this directly as a compliance failure. The client also allowed the warning to scroll off screen. Both issues required urgent remediation before approval.

Balancing Language (Risk Disclaimers)
COBS 4.2.1R
▾
3.1 What Is Balancing Language?

Balancing language refers to risk disclosures that must sit alongside benefits, features, and claims on your website. The FCA requires that promotions give a fair and prominent indication of relevant risks wherever potential benefits are described (COBS 4.2.1R). The prescribed risk warning banner alone is not sufficient — you must also include contextualised risk information throughout the body of your content.

Think of it this way: for every benefit you describe, ask yourself — "What is the corresponding risk that a consumer needs to know?" That risk information needs to appear near the benefit, not buried at the bottom of the page.

3.2 Core Principles
  • Risk disclosures must be presented with equal prominence to the benefits they accompany — matching font size, weight, and visibility.
  • Risk information must not be hidden, de-emphasised, or grouped together at the bottom of a page.
  • Where benefits are described using bold text or large font, the accompanying risk language must also be clearly visible.
  • Risk language must be specific to the feature being described — generic disclaimers are not sufficient.
3.3 Asset-Specific Risks

Where your website refers to specific types of cryptoasset, you must include risk disclosures tailored to that asset type. A general risk warning is not sufficient where specific assets or services are being promoted.

Baseline Risks (All Cryptoassets)
  • Investment risk: The value of cryptoassets can be extremely volatile. You could lose all of the money you invest.
  • Lack of protections: Cryptoassets are largely unregulated. You will not be protected by the FSCS or the FOS if something goes wrong.
  • Selling your investment: You may not always be able to sell your cryptoassets when you want.
  • Cryptoassets are complex: It may not always be clear how a cryptoasset works or what factors influence its value.
  • Diversification: It is good practice not to invest more than 10% of your money in high-risk investments.
Asset-Specific Risk Categories

The following asset types require additional tailored risk disclosures beyond the baseline risks above:

  • DeFi Tokens — Smart contract risk, regulatory risk, rug pulls/exit scams, oracle risk, protocol complexity
  • Memecoins — Extreme volatility, lack of utility, market manipulation, community-driven risks, emotional investing
  • Stablecoins — Counterparty risk, redemption risk, collateral risk, FX risk, algorithmic risk
  • Wrapped Tokens — Smart contract risk, collateral risk, custodial risk, bridging risk, price divergence
  • Security Tokens — Regulatory risk, issuer risk, liquidity risk, legal complexity, custody risk
  • Governance & Utility Tokens — Project dependency, governance concentration, volatility, functionality risk, regulatory uncertainty
3.4 Service/Product Specific Risks

Where your website promotes specific products or services, you must include risk disclosures tailored to that feature.

Fair, Clear and Not Misleading
COBS 4.2.1R
▾
4.1 The Core Standard

Under COBS 4.2.1R, every financial promotion must be fair, clear, and not misleading. This standard applies to every piece of content on your website — including headings, straplines, product descriptions, FAQs, and customer journey copy.

4.2 Substantiating Claims

Any claim you make about your product or service must be capable of being evidenced. This includes comparative claims (e.g. "faster", "lower fees"), superlative claims (e.g. "the most cost-effective"), and outcome-based claims (e.g. "save money", "grow your assets").

  • If you cannot evidence a claim, remove or rephrase it.
  • Comparative claims ("faster", "lower") must specify what they are being compared to.
  • Superlative claims ("best", "most", "leading") are high risk — they will almost always require substantiation or removal.
  • Outcome-based claims ("save money", "earn more") imply certainty and must be qualified.
4.3 Common Phrases That Require Attention
Phrase / ClaimSuggested Approach
✗ "Move your money internationally faster and with lower fees"✓ "Move your money internationally quickly and with low fees." (or provide clear, evidenced comparison)
✗ "Enjoy a fast, compliant, and hassle-free platform"✓ Remove 'compliant' unless clearly substantiated. Remove 'hassle-free' as it understates the risks involved.
✗ "[Exchange] is the trusted exchange for XYZ"✓ Either remove or link to verifiable evidence (e.g. Trustpilot rating with a direct link).

Your website must accurately represent the journey a UK user will go through. If your 'How It Works' or 'Get Started' sections present onboarding as simple or immediate, this will be flagged as misleading — because UK users must complete several mandatory steps before they can trade:

  • Self-categorisation (High Net-Worth Investor or Restricted Investor statement)
  • Appropriateness assessment
  • 24-hour cooling-off period
  • Personalised risk warning acknowledgement
Recommendation
If you want to include a 'How It Works' section, frame it as "How [Product] Works Once You Are Set Up" to avoid implying that users can access services immediately.
4.5 Testimonials and Customer Reviews

If your website displays customer testimonials or review ratings (e.g. Trustpilot), these need to be transparent in what they represent. Where they are live (linked to a Trustpilot or equivalent platform), this is generally acceptable. Where they are static elements, they must not contain sensitive words such as "secure", "guaranteed", "trusted", or "protected", and must not be misleading about the nature of the service.

The FCA has identified certain words as 'sensitive' when used in the context of cryptoasset promotions (COBS 4.2.5G). These words are flagged because they can imply a level of certainty, safety, or protection that is not appropriate given the high-risk, unregulated nature of cryptoassets.

Identifiable as a Financial Promotion
COBS 4.3.1R
▾

The Fact Sheet must be clearly identifiable as a financial promotion. This may include a ‘This is a marketing communication’ statement on each page of the document.

Suggested Wording
“This is a marketing communication.”
Placement Tip
Include the statement on every page — typically at the top or in the header — so that each page of the Fact Sheet is individually identifiable as a promotion.
2
UK Investor Information
COBS 4.15.2R & 4.15.3R
▾

The following details must be made clear to UK investors. This can be included in a dedicated ‘UK Investor’ section or integrated throughout the document, provided all disclosures are in text size relative to that used across the promotion to meet the ‘prominence’ standards (see Section 11).

2.1 Overseas Scheme Disclosure (COBS 4.15.2R(1))

It must be made clear that the scheme is authorised overseas (e.g. domiciled in Luxembourg or Ireland) but is able to market in the UK under the OFR and is not FCA-authorised.

Example Wording
“Example SICAV is authorised and regulated in [Luxembourg by the Commission de Surveillance du Secteur Financier (CSSF)] [Ireland by the Central Bank of Ireland]. It is a recognised collective investment scheme under section 264 of the Financial Services and Markets Act 2000 but is not authorised or otherwise regulated by the Financial Conduct Authority.”
2.2 FOS / FSCS Disclosures (COBS 4.15.2R(2) & 4.15.3R)

It must be disclosed to investors that they will not be able to refer complaints against the Manager or depositary to the Financial Ombudsman Service, and that they will not be covered for reimbursement under the FSCS.

Example Wording
“Example SICAV, its operator and depositary are authorised and regulated in [Luxembourg/Ireland/example]. As such, it is unlikely that the Financial Ombudsman Service will be able to consider complaints about them. Additionally, investors may not be able to claim under the Financial Services Compensation Scheme in the event of their default.”
Practical Guidance
Where space on page 1 is tight, a dedicated ‘UK Investor’ section on page 2 is an effective way to present these disclosures legibly without compressing the performance information on page 1.

General Disclaimers
COBS 4.15.2R(4)
▾
3.1 Recommendation to Seek Financial Advice

There must be a clear recommendation that the investor should seek financial advice before making any investment decisions.

Suggested Wording
“Prospective investors should consult their financial adviser before making an investment decision.”
3.2 References to Other Fund Documents

Clear references to review the Prospectus, Sub-Fund Supplement and KIID for more information should be made, including where these can be accessed (e.g. the website and language availability).

Compliance Tip
Specify the exact URL or webpage where investors can access the full suite of documents, and confirm the languages in which they are available.
4
Suitability Statement
Investor Profile & Risk Horizon
▾

This is similar to the Profile of a Typical Investor found in the Prospectus. It must address the investment time horizon and risk level in plain, accessible language. Vague language such as “long term” is not sufficient on its own — if this means 5+ years, this should be stated explicitly. The Suitability Statement will ideally sit beneath the fund’s objectives section.

Example Wording
“This Sub-Fund may be appropriate for investors who seek capital appreciation over the long term, typically meaning an investment horizon of 7 years or more. Although history has shown that shares have the potential to give better long-term returns than money market securities or bonds, they have also proved to be more volatile.”
✓ Clear & Specific✗ Vague & Non-Compliant
✓ “Suitable for investors with an investment horizon of 7 years or more who can tolerate material short-term volatility.” ✗ “Suitable for long-term investors.” (no horizon specified, no indication of risk tolerance)
✓ Places the Suitability Statement directly beneath the fund objectives. ✗ Hides the statement in a small-print disclaimer at the end of the document.
5
Risks
COBS 4.2.1R
▾
5.1 Capital Loss Warning

A clear warning that there is a potential for loss of capital must be included. This should be visually prominent and not confined to a small-print disclaimer section.

5.2 Fund-Specific Risks

If the fund invests primarily in derivatives, uses leverage, or is particularly high-risk in nature (e.g. small-cap or emerging markets exposure), clear risks pertaining to the specific characteristics of the fund should be outlined.

5.3 Equal Prominence to Benefits

Risk disclosures must be given equal prominence to any benefits or performance information — returns should not be emphasised at the expense of risk.

Common Issues to Avoid
  • ✗ Burying the capital-loss warning in a grey footnote while performance figures are emphasised in large type.
  • ✗ Generic risk language where the fund has specific, elevated risk characteristics (leverage, concentration, derivatives, emerging markets).
  • ✗ Placing all risks in a single disclaimer block at the end rather than adjacent to the content they relate to.
6
Past Performance Charts and Disclaimers
COBS 4.6.2R & 4.6.4A
▾
6.1 Net of Fees & Base Currency

Performance figures must be shown net of fees (with a disclosure identifying this clearly) and base currency must be made clear.

6.2 Past Performance Disclaimer

A past performance disclaimer must appear directly beneath each performance chart or table — not only in a footnote at the bottom of the page.

Suggested Wording
“Past performance is not a reliable indicator of future results.”
Presentation
The past performance warning should be clearly displayed and positioned directly below the past performance table or chart.
6.3 Minimum Period of Performance Data

Past performance must be presented for at least the last 5 years (or full life of the fund if shorter), unless there is less than 12 months of performance data available (COBS 4.6.2R). Where a fund has changed its strategy or benchmark, the reason for any limited history should be explained clearly to avoid the appearance of missing data.

6.4 FCA Prescribed Percentage Growth Table

There must be a percentage growth table covering at least five 12-month periods, or as many periods as performance data is available (where exceeding 12 months) (COBS 4.6.4A).

✓ Compliant Presentation✗ Non-Compliant Presentation
✓ Disclaimer directly beneath the chart, base currency labelled, net of fees confirmed. ✗ Disclaimer relegated to a single footer line in grey text at the bottom of page 2.
✓ Percentage growth table covering five discrete 12-month periods. ✗ Cumulative performance only, or selectively chosen time periods.
✓ Brief explanation where less than 5 years of data is available (e.g. strategy change, fund launch). ✗ Gaps in the performance record without explanation.

Benchmark Disclosures
COBS 4.5
▾

Where a benchmark is used, it must be clearly identified along with a summary of how it is used (target, constraining, or comparator benchmark). Where the fund is actively managed, this should be made explicit alongside the purpose of the benchmark.

Example Wording (using asterisks next to the benchmark name)
“* The Fund is actively managed and references a benchmark for asset allocation, risk measurement and comparative purposes.”

“** This factsheet also references an alternative benchmark in addition to the primary Fund’s benchmark, which is considered a suitable comparison for performance purposes due to the Fund’s investment strategy.”
Presentation
Benchmark disclosures should sit directly beneath or adjacent to the benchmark reference — not only in a general disclaimer block at the end of the document.

Fee Information
Ongoing Charges & Disclosure
▾

The Ongoing Charges Figure (OCF) should be clearly presented within the main body of the Fact Sheet — for example, in the ‘Basic Information / Fund Information’ table on the first page — rather than confined to a UK disclaimer section at the back.

Placement
Include the OCF alongside other key fund data (share class, ISIN, base currency, launch date) where retail investors expect to find it. Any additional fees and charges should be disclosed with equivalent prominence.
Common Pitfall
OCF figures buried in a small-print UK disclosure on page 2 or page 3 — when the headline fund data table on page 1 omits them — do not meet the prominence standard.

SFDR Article 6, 8 and 9 Disclosures
Sustainability Classification
▾

Where detailed information on this classification is not included in the Fact Sheet itself, there must be a clear and prominent re-direction to the Sub-Fund Supplement or Prospectus where this is covered.

What To Include
  • › The SFDR classification (Article 6, 8, or 9).
  • › A concise summary of what that classification means in practice for this fund.
  • › A clear signpost to the Sub-Fund Supplement or Prospectus for the full disclosure.

11
Prominence — A Note on Presentation
COBS 4.2.1R
▾

Prominence is one of the most frequently raised points in our reviews. A disclosure that is technically present but visually suppressed will not meet the COBS 4.2.1R requirement for communications to be fair, clear and not misleading. The following guidance should be applied across all sections above.

11.1 Font Size

Disclaimer and disclosure language should be presented in a font size broadly comparable to surrounding body text. Relegating mandatory disclosures to small-print footers or grey footnotes reduces prominence and is generally not acceptable.

11.2 Placement

Placement matters as much as size. Risk warnings, past performance disclaimers and benchmark disclosures should sit directly beneath or adjacent to the content they relate to — not only in a general disclaimer block at the end of the document.

11.3 Page Layout

Where space on page 1 is limited, a second page under a ‘UK Investor’ heading is an effective way to present the required UK disclosures in a legible format, without compressing the performance information on page 1.

11.4 Colour, Weighting & Positioning

Colour, weighting and positioning should not visually de-emphasise risk or regulatory content relative to branding, performance data or charts.

11.5 Past Performance Warning

The past performance warning (“past performance is not a reliable indicator of future results”) should be clearly displayed and positioned directly below the past performance table or chart.

Prominence Failures We Commonly See
  • ✗ UK disclosures in 6pt grey text at the base of page 2.
  • ✗ Risk warnings collapsed into a single paragraph at the end of the document, detached from the content they modify.
  • ✗ Performance figures in large bold type; risk language in thin italic grey.
  • ✗ Brand and marketing colours overwhelming the regulatory content.

A Single Promotional Package

For UK financial promotion purposes, the Prospectus, the relevant Sub-Fund Supplement and the UK Country Supplement (sometimes called a UK Addendum) are treated as a single document. Together, they form the primary documentation pack that accompanies the fund’s marketing to UK retail investors.

Where a required disclosure is not present in one of these documents, it should be clearly present in another, and any cross-references between them should be easy for the investor to follow.

Standalone Compliance
Despite the documents forming a single package, each must still be capable of operating as a compliant promotion in its own right. Where language refers to concepts such as “long-term” investment or “typical investor profile”, the document referencing those concepts should include sufficient detail, rather than relying on the investor to locate additional context in a KIID or on a website.

As the Prospectus pack is approved by a section 21 financial promotions approver, it must include the approval time stamp prominently. A statement within the UK Country Supplement is sufficient here, provided it is clearly visible and prominently displayed.

Suggested Wording (Standard)
“This financial promotion has been approved by Zeyro Ltd (FRN 1001386) on [Date].”
9.1 Scoped Approval for Umbrella Structures

Where the Prospectus covers a SICAV or umbrella structure that contains sub-funds for which Zeyro does not provide approval services (for example, institutional-only sub-funds, or sub-funds not marketed in the UK), the approval statement must make this scope clear. A blanket approval statement over the whole Prospectus would be misleading.

Suggested Wording (Scoped)
“This financial promotion has been approved by Zeyro Ltd (FRN 1001386) on [Date] with respect to the following funds only:”
  • › [Sub-Fund 1]
  • › [Sub-Fund 2]
  • › [Sub-Fund 3]
  • › [continue list as applicable]
Treatment of Updates
In many cases, subsequent updates to the Prospectus or Supplements will be treated as a variation of the original promotion and will not require a new approval time stamp. However, any material update should be shared with your reviewer, and in some cases a fresh review will be needed. Non-material updates (e.g. typographical corrections) can generally be handled without re-approval.
10
Prominence — A Note on Presentation
COBS 4.2.1R
▾

Prominence requirements apply to the Prospectus pack in the same way they apply to Fact Sheets. A disclosure that is technically present but visually suppressed will not meet the COBS 4.2.1R requirement for communications to be fair, clear and not misleading.

10.1 Font Size

Disclaimer and disclosure language should be presented in a font size broadly comparable to surrounding body text. This applies even in the Prospectus, where dense drafting can otherwise make it easy to relegate key disclosures to small print.

10.2 Signposting the UK Country Supplement

The UK Country Supplement should be positioned and labelled in a way that makes it easy for UK investors to locate — for example, signposting from the Prospectus table of contents, and a clear heading at the start of the supplement itself.

10.3 Placement of UK-Specific Disclosures

Placement matters: UK-specific disclosures should appear early in the UK Country Supplement rather than at the very end, so that an investor reading the document is exposed to the relevant warnings before reading about subscription mechanics or fees.
